# Planning a morph

The planner's job is to find a **reason** for the change and a **trail** the eye can follow. Write the answers into `plan.json`.

## 1. Inventory the two subjects

List the parts of A and B with their silhouettes, counts and materials. Note the structures that rhyme between them:

- Radial vs radial (umbrella ribs ↔ tentacles, petals ↔ wings, hourglass bulbs ↔ jellyfish bell).
- Line vs line (shaft ↔ arms, stem ↔ body, drip trail ↔ tail).
- Count mismatches. Each extra or missing part needs a fate: it merges, splits, retracts, is absorbed or is left behind.

## 2. Generate three treatments that differ in mechanism

1. **Structural.** Parts move, bend and grow into their counterparts.
2. **Material.** The object melts, flows, fuses or solidifies.
3. **Deconstructive.** The object breaks into fragments or particles, travels through a legible intermediate formation (an orbit, a column, a lattice), then reassembles.

Score each one on legibility of the middle frames, surprise, part traceability and technical risk. Pick one and keep the other two in the plan.

## 3. Devices that make the change feel inevitable

- **Environmental cause.** Something in the scene triggers the change. Rising water wakes the umbrella, and an impact spreads the ink. The background can morph too: rain slows into bubbles.
- **Pre-figuring.** The source throws out a hint of the target before the target exists. For example, splash droplets land where the feather tips will be, and drips fall where the tentacle tips will reach. The target then completes something the viewer already saw.
- **Material transport.** A small, distinctive source part travels to become a target detail. The metal ferrule melts, slides down and becomes both eyes.
- **One-to-many and many-to-one.** Show a split as a zipper that runs along the part, starting from the most distinctive end (the hook). A merge must visibly fuse, not overlap.
- **Height and depth cues.** Use a shadow that separates, a back layer drawn darker, and slight scaling toward the camera.
- **Payoff motion.** After the change, give the target the motion that only the target could make: swimming jets, wing beats, a blink. Make it at least about 25% of the duration.

## 4. Timeline shape (6–8 s at 30 fps)

| share | phase |
| --- | --- |
| 10–20% | Source at rest, with a small idle motion and the cause entering. |
| 15% | Pre-change: tension, sagging, splash, the first hint. |
| 30–35% | Transformation. Stagger parts so that something is always changing and never everything at once. |
| 10% | Settle and detail: decals, sharpening, eyes opening. |
| 25–30% | Target-specific motion. |

## 5. Risks to write down

For each risk, name the failure as a viewer would describe it ("it looks like a brain", "it has ten legs", "it pops in at 4.3 s") and give the fallback.
